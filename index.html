Povijesni okvir

Carnuntum nije bio kulisa nego grad

Na sjecištu Dunavskog limesa i Jantarnog puta Carnuntum je iz vojnog uporišta izrastao u veliko političko, trgovačko i civilno središte Panonije Superior. Uz vojni logor postojao je razvijen civilni grad, a arheološka prospekcija prepoznaje gostionice i taverne uz cestu prema amfiteatru.

Kompozitni lik

Eumachus, libertinus i tabernarius

Eumachus je kompozitni interpretativni lik tračkog podrijetla: oslobođenik, razmjerno pismen, dotaknut helenističkim obrazovanjem i trajno ogorčen raskorakom između vlastite slike o sebi i društvenog položaja. Nije identificirana povijesna osoba, nego narativni vodič sastavljen iz dokumentiranih društvenih obrazaca rimskoga svijeta.

Za šankom

Što se moglo jesti i piti?

Kruh, pogače i kaše od žitarica bili su temelj prehrane, uz leću, grašak i bob. U Carnuntumu su se jeli govedina, svinjetina, ovčetina, perad, riječna riba, školjke i puževi, ali ne svima jednako često. Pilo se lokalno ili uvozno vino, uglavnom pomiješano s vodom. Prikazane namirnice nisu jelovnik jednoga pronađenog lokala, nego povijesno moguća ponuda gradskog prostora za hranu i piće.

Metoda

Što je rekonstruirano, a što interpretirano?

Prizor spaja dokumentirani urbani kontekst Carnuntuma s tipologijom rimskih prostora za prodaju hrane i pića. Ne predstavlja digitalni preslik određenog iskopanog lokala. Kompozitni likovi, razgovor i pojedinosti radnje autorska su interpretacija utemeljena na povijesno mogućem kontekstu.

Historical setting

Carnuntum was a city, not a backdrop

At the intersection of the Danube Limes and the Amber Road, Carnuntum grew from a military base into a major political, commercial and civilian centre of Pannonia Superior. A flourishing civilian town developed beside the military complex, while archaeological prospection identifies inns and taverns along the road to the amphitheatre.

Composite character

Eumachus, libertinus and tabernarius

Eumachus is a composite interpretive character of Thracian origin: a freedman, relatively literate, touched by Hellenistic learning, and embittered by the distance between his self-image and his social position. He is not an identified historical person, but a narrative guide assembled from documented social patterns of the Roman world.

At the counter

What might people eat and drink?

Bread, flatbread and grain porridge formed the basis of the diet, alongside lentils, peas and field beans. Beef, pork, mutton, poultry, river fish, mussels and snails were eaten at Carnuntum, though not equally often by everyone. Local or imported wine was usually mixed with water. The foods shown are not the menu of one excavated establishment, but a historically plausible selection for an urban food-and-drink venue.

Method

Reconstruction or interpretation?

The scene combines Carnuntum’s documented urban context with the typology of Roman food-and-drink premises. It is not a digital replica of one excavated establishment. Its composite characters, dialogue and events are an authored interpretation grounded in a historically plausible setting.

Historischer Rahmen

Carnuntum war eine Stadt, keine Kulisse

Am Schnittpunkt von Donaulimes und Bernsteinstraße entwickelte sich Carnuntum von einem Militärstützpunkt zu einem bedeutenden politischen, wirtschaftlichen und zivilen Zentrum der Pannonia Superior. Neben dem Militärkomplex entstand eine blühende Zivilstadt; archäologische Prospektionen weisen Gasthäuser und Tavernen an der Straße zum Amphitheater nach.

Er ist keine identifizierte historische Person, sondern wurde aus dokumentierten sozialen Mustern der römischen Welt zusammengesetzt.

An der Theke

Was konnte man essen und trinken?

Brot, Fladenbrot und Getreidebrei bildeten zusammen mit Linsen, Erbsen und Ackerbohnen die Grundlage der Ernährung. In Carnuntum wurden Rind, Schwein, Schaf, Geflügel, Flussfisch, Muscheln und Schnecken gegessen — jedoch nicht von allen gleich häufig. Lokaler oder importierter Wein wurde meist mit Wasser gemischt. Die dargestellten Speisen sind nicht die Speisekarte eines ausgegrabenen Lokals, sondern eine historisch plausible Auswahl für einen städtischen Speise- und Getränkeraum.

Baza dokaza

Grad, promet i prostori hrane

Carnuntum se razvio na Dunavskom limesu i Jantarnom putu, s vojnim kompleksom i velikim civilnim gradom. Arheološka prospekcija uz cestu prema amfiteatru prepoznaje gostionice i taverne. Demo se oslanja na taj dokumentirani urbani kontekst, ali ne tvrdi da prikazuje određeni iskopani termopolij iz Carnuntuma.

Tipološka rekonstrukcija

Šank je rimski tip prostora, ne digitalni preslik

Prodajni pult, posude, hrana i zbijena društvena scena oblikovani su prema široko dokumentiranoj tipologiji rimskih prostora za prodaju hrane i pića. Njihov točan raspored, boje, namirnice i gustoća ljudi u ovom prizoru izvedbene su odluke, a ne arheološki plan jednog lokala u Carnuntumu.

Hrana i piće

Carnuntumski nalazi, tipološka interpretacija

Arheobotanički i arheozoološki nalazi iz Carnuntuma potvrđuju različite žitarice i mahunarke te goveda, svinje, ovce, perad, riječnu ribu, školjke i puževe. Nalazi sjemenki grožđa, vinogradarskog alata i amfora potvrđuju potrošnju, lokalni uzgoj i uvoz vina. Međutim, nije poznat inventar jednoga termopolija koji bi odgovarao ovom prizoru. Pult s ugrađenim posudama tipološka je analogija s drugih rimskih lokaliteta, osobito Pompeja.

Evidence base

City, mobility and food premises

Carnuntum developed on the Danube Limes and the Amber Road, with a military complex and a major civilian city. Archaeological prospection along the road to the amphitheatre identifies inns and taverns. The demo draws on this documented urban setting but does not claim to depict one specific excavated thermopolium at Carnuntum.

Typological reconstruction

A Roman type of space, not a digital replica

The counter, vessels, food and crowded social scene follow the broadly documented typology of Roman food-and-drink premises. Their exact arrangement, colours, foods and number of people are performance choices rather than the archaeological plan of one establishment at Carnuntum.

Food and drink

Carnuntum evidence, typological interpretation

Archaeobotanical and archaeozoological evidence from Carnuntum documents several grains and pulses as well as cattle, pigs, sheep, poultry, river fish, mussels and snails. Grape seeds, viticultural tools and amphorae attest consumption, local cultivation and wine imports. No inventory is known from a single thermopolium corresponding to this scene. The counter with embedded vessels is a typological analogy drawn from other Roman sites, especially Pompeii.

Composite interpretive character

Eumachus is not an identified historical person

His Thracian origin is grounded in documented Thracian mobility across the Roman Empire. A freedman and tabernarius is historically possible, but his minor-aristocratic origin before enslavement, relative literacy, contact with Hellenistic learning, self-image and bitterness are narrative components. His name is not a prosopographical identification from Carnuntum.

Speisen und Getränke

Befunde aus Carnuntum, typologische Interpretation

Archäobotanische und archäozoologische Befunde aus Carnuntum belegen verschiedene Getreide und Hülsenfrüchte sowie Rinder, Schweine, Schafe, Geflügel, Flussfische, Muscheln und Schnecken. Traubenkerne, Weinbaugeräte und Amphoren bezeugen Konsum, lokalen Anbau und Weinimporte. Ein Inventar aus einem einzelnen Thermopolium, das dieser Szene entspräche, ist jedoch nicht bekannt. Die Theke mit eingelassenen Gefäßen ist eine typologische Analogie zu anderen römischen Fundorten, insbesondere Pompeji.
